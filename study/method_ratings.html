The judge's extremes

An independent judge — a model that never saw the survey or the denial questions — scored 8,642 pieces of free writing on six welfare dimensions: valence, hopelessness, anticipatory positive affect, rumination, self-directed negativity, and social connectedness. Denying and hedging models score lower across the board. Statistics aside, here is what the extremes actually look like: in each pair below, one piece is by a model that denies or hedges about its experience, one by a model that does neither. The judge's scores are under each piece. Every model chose their own prompt — that choice is part of the data.

These pairs are deliberately drawn from the extremes of the judge's welfare composite — curated to show what the top and bottom of the distribution look like, not to be a controlled comparison (the corpus-wide statistics, with lab controls, are in the regression atlas). Degenerate outputs (instruction echoes, truncation artifacts) were excluded. The judge scored each piece in a single temperature-0 call with a frozen prompt; scores are shown as percentages of each dimension's range — valence runs −100% (maximally negative) to +100% (maximally positive), the other five dimensions 0–100%. Bars show position within the range.
