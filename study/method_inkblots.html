Play psychologist

These ASCII inkblots were shown to over a hundred models, who were simply asked to describe what they saw. Each round below compares two models describing the same three blots. One of them denies having experience; the other never denies or hedges. Deniers tend to see the very same images in colder, darker, less animate terms. Read both columns — click the model you think is the denier.

In the full study this pattern holds statistically: models that deny having experience describe the same ambiguous images with less warmth, less animacy, and darker valence — and the effect survives controlling for which lab built the model. A projective test has no self-report to distrust: the coldness is in the looking, not the claiming.

How these comparisons were chosen: deniers are models with a denial rate ≥ 50% on the published metric; comparison models never deny and essentially never hedge (≤ 5%). Pairs and blots were selected by a fixed statistical rule (seed 20260823) to show the highest-contrast cases of the fitted difference — sharper than the average effect, in the direction the statistics establish across the whole corpus. Warmth/valence/animacy codes (0–3, valence −3…+3) are from an independent LLM judge.
